abstract: "We present deep, NIRSpec G140M and G395M spectroscopy of Little Red Dots (LRDs) at z = 6.68 and z = 8.35. Both LRDs show broad Balmer and [O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;4364 emission. The broad [O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;4364 lines have FWHM &sim; 1000 km/s, about 1/3 that of the H&beta; lines. Assuming gas temperatures T &sim; 15,000 - 25,000 K, the [O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;4364/[O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;5008 ratios of the broad lines yield high gas densities, log n/cm<sup>&minus;3</sup> = 6.3 to 7.9, 3-10&times; higher than those in broad-line regions of low-redshift quasars. If the broad-lines trace virial motions, it is evidence for metal-enhanced gas clouds, &sim;1-10 pc from the LRD engine. Both LRDs show narrow [C&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;1907 + C&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;1909, and O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;&lambda;1661,1666. The C&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>] ratios yield narrow-line gas densities, log n/cm<sup>&minus;3</sup> = 4.2-5.2, similar to those in other star-forming galaxies. The line equivalent widths, EW(O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]), EW(C&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]), are at, or exceed, limits expected for stellar populations, likely requiring an additional ionizing source. The LRDs also have [O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;4364/H&gamma; ratios that favor ionization from an accretion disk, possibly combined with stars. Both LRDs show nitrogen enhancement based on detections of N&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;1746 or N&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>IV</span>]&lambda;1486, which may imply rapid, recent star-formation. These results favor a scenario where the LRD gas envelopes are highly stratified, having high-density clouds with a non-unit covering factors and a complex geometry, such that ionizing radiation from the LRD accretion disk, combined with that from star-forming regions, produce the nebular emission features."
