summary: An invited seminar talk about the collaboration SIGNALS. Using data from the SIGNALS survey and the SITELLE spectrograph, I highlight how we analyze HII regions to study their physical properties, kinematics, and environmental influences.
abstract: |
  H&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>II</span> regions, clouds of ionized gas and dust created by massive stars within young stellar clusters, serve as fundamental laboratories for understanding star formation and galaxy evolution. By analyzing their ionized gas properties, we can trace the conditions that shape stare formation across different galactic environments. The **Star-formation, Ionized Gas, and Nebular Abundances Legacy Survey (SIGNALS)** is investigating these processes by studying more than 50,000 H&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>II</span> regions across 35 nearby galaxies at an average spatial resolution of 20 pc. SIGNALS utilizes the imaging Fourier transform spectrograph SITELLE at the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope to observe strong emission lines—[O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>II</span>]&lambda;3727, H&beta;, [O&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>III</span>]&lambda;&lambda;4959,5007, [N&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>II</span>]&lambda;&lambda;6548,6584, H&alpha;, and [S&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>II</span>]&lambda;&lambda;6717,6731—allowing us to derive key physical properties of the ionized gas. In collaboration with the SIGNALS team, we are developing methods to systematically identify and analyze H&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>II</span> regions. In this presentation, I will highlight these techniques and showcase results for selected galaxies, including H&nbsp;<span style='font-size: 0.8em;'>II</span> region populations, metallicity distributions, luminosity functions, and kinematic properties in diverse environments.
